# Steward messaging · the source of truth (4 Oct 2026)

Jonathan approved this language on 4 Oct: "this is all gold, I want this language everywhere." Every page, help article, empty state, onboarding screen, demo and email reads from this. Voice: invitation, not pressure. These are people doing sacred work on thin budgets. No em dashes. No competitor names. No unsourced statistics.

## The vision

Small nonprofits run on relationships and lose them to turnover, spreadsheets and sheer overload. Someone gives from the heart, and a year later nobody remembers to call. Steward is the institutional memory of generosity: every donor, every conversation, every promise, held gently, so that no one who ever believed in the cause is forgotten by it.

## The headline and the line under it

Headline: **Raise more from the people who already believe in you.**
Under it: Your reports tell you what happened. Steward tells you why, and who to call tomorrow.
The feeling: Imagine never losing track of a single person who believes in what you do.

## The six pillars

**1. Never lose a single believer.**
Every donor is someone who raised their hand for your mission. Steward notices who has gone quiet, not as a report you have to pull but as a tap on the shoulder. When you ask why, it shows the reasons in dollars, and every number opens the gifts and conversations behind it. Then it tells you who to call tomorrow morning and puts each step on your list for the day it's due. No one who ever believed in the cause is forgotten by it.

**2. Technology that knows its place.**
Every nonprofit leader has the same nightmare: software that says the wrong thing to the right donor. So we built the opposite. Steward remembers everything and assumes nothing. It drafts the note, suggests the call and prepares the brief, and it never sends, publishes or acts without your yes. It's the staff member who does all the homework and none of the talking out of turn. The relationship stays yours. It just never has to rely on your memory alone.

**3. One home for everyone who believes.**
Right now your people are scattered: donors in a spreadsheet, volunteers in another app, gala guests on a clipboard. But they're not separate people. The woman who gave $500 also worked Saturdays and brought two friends to the gala. Steward keeps one story per person, every gift, every hour, every conversation, so when you reach out you know the whole relationship, not just the last transaction. Your community isn't donors plus volunteers. It's people, whole people, with whole histories.

**4. Numbers you can stand behind.** *(drafted by Cowork to complete the set; Jonathan to confirm)*
When the board asks how the year went, you should be able to answer without a weekend in spreadsheets. Every figure in Steward opens the gifts behind it and adds up to the cent, so you can say it out loud and know it's true. Your story, told in numbers you trust.

**5. You don't have to tear anything down.**
The last thing an overstretched team needs is a migration project. So Steward doesn't ask for one. It connects to the tools you already use, your payments, your books, your email, and quietly watches them, so nothing slips through the cracks between systems. You keep your world. We just make sure nothing in it gets lost.

**6. A partner, not a vendor.** *(drafted by Cowork to complete the set; Jonathan to confirm)*
You'll talk to the person who built it. Month to month, no platform fee on your gifts, and Steward never holds or moves your money. If something isn't right, it gets fixed in days, not quarters. We'd rather earn your trust every month than lock it in for a year.

## When they ask "how do you do that?"

"Three things, all from your own records. First, Steward notices who's gone quiet, not as a report you have to pull, but as a tap on the shoulder: these eleven people gave last spring and haven't this year. Second, when you ask why, why the appeal came in under, why she stopped giving, it shows you the reasons in dollars, and every number opens the actual gifts and conversations behind it, so you can trust it. Third, it tells you who to call tomorrow morning and why, and puts each step on your list for the day it's due. It never sends anything on its own. It's your judgment, with a perfect memory."

That's two minutes in a demo. Concrete enough to believe, gentle enough for the room.

## For the careful ones

They're not stuck, they're stewards of other people's generosity. Honor that: "You've spent years building these relationships. Steward doesn't replace any of that. It just makes sure nobody you've ever thanked falls through the cracks again."

## The opening gift

The free audit (Lost & Found) is a gift, not a hook: ten minutes, no pitch, their own file. Not "you're losing $47,000." Instead: "Here are the people who stood with you last year. Let's make sure none of them slip away unnoticed."

## Words to use and avoid

Use: believers, people who stood with you, gone quiet, tap on the shoulder, your judgment with a perfect memory, whole people, whole histories, nothing slips through the cracks.
Avoid: money left on the table, leads, conversion, churn, users (say people, donors, your team), recovered, won back, revenue leakage, AI-powered.

## Keep it true

Every claim must be true on prod the day it ships. "Never sends without your yes" means the Agent and drafts; receipts and sequences an org has turned on are the org's own yes, so say "Steward never sends anything you haven't approved." "Connects to your books" only once QuickBooks is approved by Intuit and live for customers; until then say "payments and email". Prices stay off this copy until pricing is decided.
