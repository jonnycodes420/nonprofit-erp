# How Steward is built

Most donor software answers what happened. Steward answers why it happened and what to do next.

A report that ends in a chart leaves the work to the fundraiser. A screen in Steward ends in a decision: a name, a reason, and a step she can take today. The dashboard is the starting point. The finish line is the call she makes tomorrow morning.

## Parity first

Steward wins by being just as good as the leading platform, at a better price, with better service. Every screen a buyer expects from the market leader should exist in Steward and be as easy to find. That means the same intelligence and the same breadth: everything the leader knows about a person, Steward knows too, across giving, volunteering, events, email and conversations. We say it in our own words (a plain word and its reasons, not a meter), but we never know less. Where Steward answers "why" and "who next", that's on top of parity, never instead of it.

## Nobody types into Steward (18 Oct 2026)

The development director does the relationships. Steward does the paperwork. Every CRM is a form someone has to fill in, and nobody fills it in. So Steward writes the record itself from what already happens: gifts from the tools the org uses, checks photographed on a phone, letters and pledge forms in email, meetings and memos spoken after lunch. Before building anything, ask which chore it makes disappear. A build that only adds a screen to fill in is going the wrong way.

The promise we sell is the zero-day thank-you: every gift logged, receipted and personally thanked the day it lands. The number is gift-to-thanked time.

**Two speeds.** Low-stakes things (a note, an address, a meeting summary, a document filed) happen on their own, listed under "Done for you" with Undo. Anything involving money, and anything personal or sensitive, waits as a card for one tap. Nothing reaches a donor without a person's yes.

## The rules every build follows

1. **Every screen ends in a decision.** If a page shows a number that's off, it also says why, who it's about and what to do. "Spring appeal is down $4,200" isn't finished. "Down $4,200, mostly because 11 of last year's donors haven't given yet; here are the five to call first" is.
2. **Every reason is a fact with its rows.** Steward never guesses at a cause it can't show. "14 of last year's donors haven't given yet" opens those 14. "People are tired of email" is not a reason Steward can give.
3. **Rank, then commit.** When there are several reasons, Steward ranks them by dollars and says which matters most. It doesn't list ten things and leave the choosing to her.
4. **The recommendation is one step, and a person takes it.** Steward plans the call, drafts the note, puts it on the Thread. It never sends, charges or contacts a donor on its own. The one exception is a sequence the org's own staff wrote and turned on.
5. **Say what we can't see.** The data shows a donor stopped after a declined card; it can't show that she lost her job. When the answer is thin, Steward says so.
6. **AI writes the sentence, not the facts.** The causes are computed from the org's own data. The model only turns computed facts into plain words, and it goes through the AI switch. With AI off, the same answer appears in plain template sentences.
7. **The questions people ask are the roadmap.** Steward logs every question asked (the question only, never donor data). What it can't answer yet is what we build next.
8. **Capture is careful.** Health, family crises and other personal details are never filed silently. They're held back as a neutral line until a person chooses to save them. People marked as clients are never captured at all.

## How to demo it

Don't open with the reports. Ask Steward, live, why last year's appeal did better and who to call tomorrow morning. If the answer is a name, a reason and a step, that's the product. Then snap a check or tell Steward about the meeting you're in, and show it land before you leave the room.
