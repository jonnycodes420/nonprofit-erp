## SCALE-5 · Smart moves stop holding the pool, and background sweeps skip orgs nobody uses (2026-11-17)

**Why.** Prod's /ops on 17 Nov, "Who held the database" over 24 hours: `job processSmartMoves` 15,208 connection-seconds (56.6%, up to 4 at once), `job queue (claim, heartbeat, reap)` 6,808 (25.3%), `job numberCheckNightly` 9.9%. "Most database connections in use" was red at 100% for the day and the week. The nightly load failed on `GET /donors` (p95 1,095 ms, max 10,032 ms, one 5xx). `processSmartMoves` ran every five minutes over `SELECT id FROM orgs WHERE onboarding_complete=1`: every onboarded org, archived and test orgs of 25,000 people included, from scratch, 288 times a day.

**Live orgs.** `orgKind.isLiveOrg`: not archived, and of a kind a person looks at (customer, prospect trial, demo, from `inferKind`, so a sweep and /admin never disagree), CREO kept by id. `server/orgs/liveOrgs.js` reads it once a minute per process. Every per-org background sweep reads it (seventeen, listed in `tests/scale5.test.js` §3). Not changed, on purpose: billing (record bands, trial reminders and expiry), deletion and retention (trash, asset purge, Atlas expiry), a send a person scheduled (campaigns, sequences, journey auto-sends, dunning, card expiry, pledge-reminder sends), and work that only exists where an org connected something (giving sources, QuickBooks, Xero, Mailchimp, webhooks). Mailbox sync is filtered (the brief named it). A call scoped to one org is never filtered.

**Smart moves for what changed.** `org_changes` already records every write to every org table, by statement trigger, with its transaction id (SCALE-2's orgSummary). The tick keeps a mark (`pg_snapshot_xmin` at its start) and works only live orgs with a change to a table the lapse rule reads at or above the last mark: an open transaction is never missed, and a change seen twice costs one empty pass. One org at a time, each on one pinned connection. An org whose floor (orgSummary's housekeeping) rose past the mark counts as changed. The nightly job (hourly, long) sweeps each live org once in its own 2–6 AM to the end of its list: lapses that only the date makes are found there, not within five minutes of midnight. The moves themselves are the same function's; the suite runs a scripted day both ways and compares moves and stages byte for byte.

**The queue.** The claim was a transaction of 3 statements empty and 8 with a job, four lanes a second; the scheduler sent 2 statements per periodic job every ten seconds. Now: one `UPDATE … FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED` per claim (claims in a process one at a time, so lanes never race for an org's last place); one upsert per new definition and one due check for all; one heartbeat write per process for every run it holds; prune hourly; an empty queue asked by one lane with a wait that doubles from `JOB_POLL_MS` to `JOB_POLL_MAX_MS` (twice the poll, 2 s). A database wake (`NOTIFY steward_jobs` from a trigger, heard on one listening connection outside the pool, `server/jobs/wake.js`) was built and measured: `/donors` did not move, but a gift's POST p95 was 671 ms with it against 495 ms without, and slower than main in each paired run. It ships off (`JOB_WAKE=on` to use it); the brief's "wake at once" holds for work the worker enqueues itself, and work the web enqueues waits at most 2 s.

**Planted, each red:** isLiveOrg ignoring archived; smart moves over every onboarded org; the tick working every org; the tick blind to opportunity changes (needed the "settle before each write" step: a tick's own moves are changes and masked it at first); dataHealth's nightly without the filter; a claim of three statements; every lane polling at full speed. The first byte-identical compare read `substring(id from $2)` as a regex (null for every person) and passed by accident; caught by the planted run, cast to int.

Suite: `tests/scale5.test.js`.

**The org-specific ratchet** for `orgKind.js` went from 1 to 2 lines: the kept list names CREO (the brief: by id, as ADMIN-CLEAN keeps it) and `org_stagewalk`, staging's walk org. That org's name ("Staging walk") reads as a fixture, so without it the sweeps would skip the very org the fleet writes in to check them.

**What CI caught.** The first one-statement claim counted the per-org cap inside the candidate WHERE, so Postgres ran that count for every due waiting row before sorting. In a shard's busy queue each claim became a scan per row, and `flows2`'s Raised drawer sat on "Reading the rows…" (red twice in CI, reproduced locally; green with main's jobQueue.js). The claim now takes the first `CLAIM_TRIES` due rows by the cheap filters and order, counts running jobs once for just those orgs, and locks the chosen row last. It is still one statement. `flows2` is green again (117 → 59 s for the suite).
