## NUMBERS-4 · The rest of the copies, one rule per donor label, and big imports that finish (2026-11-01)

Brief: a 25,000-person import that finishes, "Donor since" on seed 695574508, one rule per donor label (lapse, major and the tags, people and households, the suggested ask, a stable last gift), the remaining copies MAP-1 listed, a before-and-after on the real-shaped files, and (the architect's amendment) every hand-written money sum classed, with the ratchet widened to all server code.

**The 25k import.** It reproduced only on a database that already holds other orgs: on a near-empty one main took 17 s. Inside the import's transaction a fresh org is not in the planner's statistics, so every join was planned as one person. `KEEP_IMPORTED_TOTAL` joined `donors` to itself (2 min 14 s and 5 min 24 s in two runs), and the scoped recompute read the org's every gift once per person (5 min 53 s; a first fix that still joined took 25 s a batch, its plan in the scratch Postgres log through `auto_explain`). Both statements now have no join (each person read once, their gifts one `(org, person)` index lookup) and run 500 people at a time. A combined import of 1,000 people or more ANALYZEs `donors` and `gifts` once it commits, because the background rhythm and count jobs after it had the same plan (7 minutes and more). The whole-org recompute takes 0.45 s for 25,000 people. perf-app gained `tests/lib/workbookImportLeg.js`, the workbook imported the way the screen sends it, with pledges, merges and background work: red on main (no answer in 120 s), 43.8 s here. The workbook screen shows one progress bar over its three steps and stays one transaction and one Undo.

**Seed 695574508** was the checker's key, not the app: a matching gift credit dated before an imported first gift. Every surface leaves a credit row out of "first gift"; `check-numbers.js` counted it. `firstGift.js` now owns the old system's first gift and the on-file aggregate, and the seed's sequence runs as a fixed case every time (§3a).

**One rule per label.** `shared/numbers/lapse.js` (one SQL fragment; `lapseRule.js` keeps the pure rule because the browser bundles it), `tags.js` (Major is the Giving levels cut on the last 12 months; the Insights ladder had its own line, an any-single-gift rule and a top-5% fallback, so Home's Major rung held 22 people on real-shaped where the tag held 5; now both say 5), `households.js`, `ask.js` (one suggested ask for the profile and every card; a sponsorship's price and a pledge's balance keep theirs), `pledges.js` (a pledge's paid, 17 copies, every one counting credit rows) and a stable last and first gift (`gifts.import_row`, a new nullable column). The before-and-after found two of its own mistakes and they were fixed: the first gift still fell to a random id on a tie, and one day's gifts, ordered largest first, read as "rising" and stepped an ask up a rung.

**The money sums.** `docs/architecture/money-sums.md` classes all 248 (after this build) by hand: 125 are legitimately their own, 123 are totals a person sees; 43 of those were moved this build and 50 (and 2 partial) are left, in order, for NUMBERS-5. `route-gift-sql` counts all server code (main 367 lines, here 348) and `number-copies` is new (a planted second "major" rule went red).

Left in `docs/defects.md`: "Import a spreadsheet" refuses the 25k workbook at 5 MB and, given room, reads it as one sheet of 1,101 people (P1; the limit was tried at 64 MB and put back), the 50 money sums, the client-shared report builder's pledge paid, Lost & Found's browser-side last gift, Insights' unit-based retained stats, `/donors/counts` counting sample people, and three rising donors whose Home ask steps up while the profile holds.
