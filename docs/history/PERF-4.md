## PERF-4 · Every main page is ready in under 2 seconds, measured the way a person feels it (2026-11-05)

**Why.** Muse's Walk B (5 Nov) timed seven pages on prod: none was ready when navigation returned, each about 4 s.

**The page-ready mark** (`client/src/lib/pageReady.js`, installed by main.jsx on an app path with a session). A page is ready when the content column (`.app-content`) has text, nothing in it is a skeleton, a "Loading…" or "Reading…" line, busy or `-loading`, and no GET to the API is in flight (the AI stream and the beacon excepted), held for 150 ms; the time is when that first held, from the navigation's start or, for a move inside the app, from the address change. It is a `performance.mark`, `window.__stewardReady` (with `window.__stewardInFlight()` to name what a slow page waited on), and one beacon, `POST /perf/page-ready` `{ page, ms, fresh }` (`server/perf/pageTiming.js`): a fixed page name, the milliseconds, first-after-load or moved. Kept a day in the web process's memory, never in a table, its audit row skipped (a time changes nothing). /ops shows Page timing beside the walk trend (median and 95th, first and moved). Super-admin sign-in needs two-factor, so the card was checked by rendering OpsView with a real summary, not in a browser.

**Measured** (`tests/lib/pageReadyLeg.js`, table: `scripts/perf/page-ready.js`). Fixtures from SCALE-1's seeder plus LOAD-1's journeys, scores and rhythms: 1,000 people with 508 gifts this year (the walk's size), and 25,000 people. Cold is a fresh POST /auth/login in a fresh browser with its cache off; warm is the page again. Desktop, 40 ms, 10 Mbps. On CI's runner main was already under 2 s everywhere (Donors 1.2 s, profile 1.5 s); the walk's 4 s is prod's round trip. At a 150 ms round trip main's Donors (2.23 s) and profile (2.43 s) were over 2 s; after, 1.91 and 1.96.

**What waited, and the fixes.**
- Every page but Home asked for its code only once App's code had downloaded and run, and its reads only once that code had mounted (1.4 to 2 s after the boot's reads had answered, at 150 ms). app.html's preloader now preloads the page's own chunk and what it imports (`data-page-need`, `PAGE_CHUNKS` in `client/scripts/prerender.mjs`; the CSP hash moved), and main.jsx asks for the page's first reads in one bundle with the boot's (`client/src/lib/pageReads.js`; the paths joined `routes/homeBundle.js` HOME_READS; `/stripe/status`, which asks Stripe, is left to the page). Tried and not kept: importing the pages' chunks from main.jsx, which made Rollup split App's shared code into a dozen small chunks and grew Home's first look from 239 to 260 KB (perf-app caught it).
- A profile read its record, rail, journeys and status from two panels each; the second reader asked the network again a few milliseconds after the first was handed the bundle's answer (seven reads, one of them 250 KB, in a second wave). `api.js`: an answer just handed from the park is shared with a second reader for 1.5 s, as an answer in flight is; a write forgets it.
- Donors downloaded the spreadsheet reader (113 KB gzipped) and the import screens with its list: through `DonorImport.jsx`, re-exported but used by nobody, and two helpers taken from it. The drop target is `SheetDropTarget.jsx` (re-exported by SpreadsheetImport), the reader, the old-reports wizard and the grant file reader load when their window opens, and the merge modal comes from its own module: Donors 226 → 188 KB gzipped, its first look about 150 KB lighter.

**Left.** Donors' list still waits for `/donors/list-setup` (its columns), and the giving report's second year for its first; asking either earlier means copying the page's rules into the boot. On the 25,000-person fixture Settings waits about 1 s on `/stripe/status` (a made-up Stripe account and a Stripe address nothing answers); on prod it is one real Stripe call.

**Check** (in `tests/perf-app.spec.js`, strict on CI too): each of the seven ready under 2 s after a fresh sign-in, median of 3; a page over the bar is measured once more and that reading is held to it (FLAKE-1). Planted: Settings' first reads one after another, at a 150 ms round trip: 1.56 s → 3.46 s twice, red. hotfix1-profile §1b now fails the record's first read wherever it is asked (its bundle too): the profile no longer makes the second request it used to fail.
