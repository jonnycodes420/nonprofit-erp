## SCALE-4 · Who fills the pool, the number check off the web, long jobs in their own lanes, and replica advice that counts connections (2026-11-16)

**Why.** Prod's /ops (15 Nov): "Most database connections in use" red at 100% for a day and a week, a 39-minute job wait in the week, request p95 1.3 to 1.8 s on live replicas, the nightly load failing on three-year, annual and `/donors`. The card advised a second web replica that would need 58 connections of the pooler's 40 during a deploy. Nothing on the card said whose the connections were.

**Who held the database** (`server/perf/holders.js`). Every connection passes `requestLimits.checkout` / `release`, so each is put down to a holder there: a request by its route pattern (`GET /reports/:key`, never a path or an id; a connection taken before routing goes to the route when the request ends), background work by `holders.as(name)`: `job <name>` for every queued run (`jobQueue.runOne`), `tick <name>` for bootGate's ticks (`{ name }`) and per-boot jobs, "job queue (claim, heartbeat, reap)" for the queue itself. Per holder: connection-milliseconds, most at once, uses. Each five-minute capacity sample writes its top 25 to `capacity_holders`; the card shows the top 8 over 24 hours.

**The number check** was on the web's hourly tick, outside any request, eight people at a time with three reads each: the per-request cap never applied. Measured on a 14-person fixture without the pin: 10 connections at once (the whole pool), 453 checkouts. Now it is a queued long job (`numberCheckNightly`) on the worker, each org on one connection (`withPinnedClient` in `server/db/query.js`: query/run/querySetwise go to one client, statements queued in order, no transaction), one org at a time with `NUMBER_CHECK_PAUSE_MS` (2 s) between. Orgs marked test are skipped at night; their latest on-demand check (`POST /ops/numbers/check-org`, "Check now" on the Numbers card) stands in the digest line, which keeps its shape. Counts byte-identical to `checkOrg` (scale4 §2).

**Long jobs.** Every whole-platform pass (`recomputeScores`, `snapshotAllOrgMetrics`, `refreshStoredDrift`, `orgSummaryCheck`, `storedReportsNightly`, `runDataHealthNightly`, the number checks) is declared `long`, and any job type whose run took 60 s or more in a process becomes long there. Long jobs take at most `WORKER_CONCURRENCY - JOB_SHORT_LANES` (4 - 1) lanes; claims in a process go one at a time so two lanes cannot both take the last long place. See the report for the reproduction with prod's 17 ms per statement.

**Reports.** Three-year sums each person's gifts first (`givingBetween.SPAN_SUM_OF_MONEY`, the money rule once per gift in the WHERE) and joins people after, feeding the last sort in id order as the grouped read did, so ties keep their order: ~190 → ~100 ms at 25,000 people. Reports share one answer per statement (`requestLimits.memoize`, as bundles do). `gifts_org_day` (org_id, LEFT(date, 10)) lets retention's two windows read the window, not every gift (`server/db/scale4Indexes.js`, CONCURRENTLY, not awaited, then ANALYZE). 160 report answers byte-identical to main (8 reports × 4 orgs × 5 year asks). The per-person per-year rollup SCALE-3 planned was not needed.

**The card.** The replica advice checks the budget first (`server/perf/connectionBudget.js`): web replicas × (pool + extra) × 2 + the worker, against the smaller of `SHOW max_connections` and `POOLER_SIZE`. When one more replica does not fit, the sentence says so and what comes first (a smaller DB_POOL_MAX, with what it costs per request, or more pooler room): `docs/needs-jonathan/SCALE-4.md`. Replicas with no sample in 24 hours fold into one line per measure ("26 earlier replicas, last seen …") whose 7-day value still counts, only from rows in those 7 days.

Suite: `tests/scale4.test.js` (§1–§5, each planted red).
