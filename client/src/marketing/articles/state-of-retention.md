Every quarter the Fundraising Effectiveness Project, a collaboration of AFP and GivingTuesday, publishes giving data from thousands of nonprofits. Its Q4 2025 report covered the full year, and the headline was mixed: dollars rose 5.0%, but the number of donors fell 3.6%.

## Fewer people, bigger checks

Overall [donor retention](/glossary/donor-retention-rate) edged up to 43.3%. That still means more than half of the people who gave in 2024 did not give in 2025. Growth came mostly from larger gifts while small donors kept falling away.

::quote 0

## The second gift

The gap between new and repeat donors is the most useful number in fundraising. In FEP's Q4 2024 report, 19.4% of [new donors](/glossary/first-year-retention) gave again, compared with 69.2% of [repeat donors](/glossary/repeat-donor-retention). Get the second gift and a donor is far more likely to stay.

## What moves the number

The research points to the same few habits: a fast, personal thank-you, proof that the gift mattered, and a renewal ask timed to the donor's own rhythm. Penelope Burk found that a thank-you call within 48 hours led to gifts 39% larger the next time. Adrian Sargeant's work suggests a 10% lift in retention can raise the [lifetime value](/glossary/donor-lifetime-value) of a donor file by up to 200%.

::quote 1

## What a small shop can do this week

Pull last year's donors who have not given this year: your [LYBUNT](/glossary/lybunt) list. Call the top twenty. Thank every new gift within two days. Put a first-year plan on the calendar for every new donor. Steward does the sorting and the reminders. You do the part that only a person can.
